Getting from Ōtsu to your Kyoto sights

Kyoto → Yamashina → Ōtsu → Zeze → Ishiyama → Seta describes selected stations on the JR Biwako corridor. You do not change trains at each one. For your hotel-to-Kyoto journey, travel in the reverse direction, westbound. Some faster services skip Zeze and Seta; choose a service that stops at your hotel station.

Where you want to goSimple route from Ōtsu
Kyoto Station / teamLabJR Seta, Ishiyama or Ōtsu → Kyoto Station. Then walk/taxi to teamLab. Seta → Kyoto is about 17–20 min on the train; hotel-to-attraction time is longer.
Gion / Sanjō / HigashiyamaJR hotel station → Yamashina → change to Tozai subway westbound → Sanjo Keihan or Higashiyama. For Gion, walk south from Sanjō or use Keihan one stop to Gion-Shijō. No Kyoto Station detour needed.
Central Kyoto, from a Hama-Ōtsu-area stayBiwako-Hamaōtsu / Kamisakaemachi → Keishin train through onto Tozai subway → Sanjo Keihan. About 25 min from Hama-Ōtsu, no train change on a through service. For farther-west stops such as Karasuma Oike/Nijō, check the train’s destination.
Fushimi Inari / ArashiyamaJR → Kyoto; then JR Nara Line local to Inari, or JR Sagano Line to Saga-Arashiyama. Kyoto Station is a useful hub for these days.

Kyoto Station is not on the outskirts. It is on the southern side of the central urban area. Shijō–Kawaramachi/Gion is farther northeast and is a different shopping/dining centre. Kyoto Station → Shijō is only two stops on the Karasuma subway. Your attractions are spread out: teamLab near Kyoto Station, Inari south, Arashiyama west.

For your 10-minute walking rule: Smile Seta and le Lac Ishiyama comfortably fit it. If a similarly priced suitable room appears near JR Ōtsu, it shortens the JR commute; a stay near Keihan Hama-Ōtsu/Kamisakaemachi instead favours direct central-Kyoto access. These are different stations, so check the hotel’s actual walking route.
